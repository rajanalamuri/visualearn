The idea

Information can be found. Judgement has to be grown.

The great algorithms are not just computer code. They are the best ways people have found to think through a hard problem: cut it in half, put it in order, look near before far, find the simple pieces underneath. Children who learn these habits can use them for the rest of their lives, with or without a screen.

Why it works

Built on how brains learn

  • Guess before you see

    Every step asks the child to predict what happens next. Trying to come up with an answer, even a wrong one, makes the real answer stick far better than just watching.

    Generation effect (Slamecka & Graf, 1978); retrieval practice (Roediger & Karpicke, 2006); errorful generation (Kornell, Hays & Bjork, 2009).
  • Pictures and words together

    Each idea is shown as a moving picture with one short sentence beside it, never a wall of text or a busy screen.

    Dual coding (Paivio, 1971); multimedia learning (Mayer, 2001).
  • Hands, not just eyes

    Every lesson ends with something to do with cards, coins, paper or people. Ideas you have acted out with your own hands are the ones you remember.

    The approach behind CS Unplugged (Bell, Witten & Fellows), used in classrooms around the world.
  • Short, then stop

    Ten minutes, then a daily limit. Short sessions spread over days beat long ones, and they leave time for sleep, play and people.

    Spacing effect (Cepeda et al., 2006).

These are well-studied principles of learning. VisuaLearn itself has not been tested in a trial yet. When it is, we will publish the results here, good or bad.
