Balanced trees (AVL)

Advanced · ages 13+

A search tree is fast only if it stays bushy. Adding numbers in order makes a lopsided stick. An AVL tree checks each node, and when one side gets two levels taller than the other, it rotates to fix it.

Play this lesson

How it works

  1. add the number like a normal search tree
  2. going back up, check each node: left height − right height
  3. if it is 2 or −2, this node is lopsided
  4. rotate it to even the sides out
  5. the tree is balanced again

Balanced trees (AVL) Advanced

Tip the tree

You need Paper, a pencil and 6 numbers in increasing order · Time 15 minutes

  1. Draw a search tree by adding 10, 20, 30 in that order. It is a stick leaning right.
  2. The top node has nothing on its left but two levels on its right. Rotate: make 20 the top, with 10 on its left and 30 on its right.
  3. Keep adding 40, 50, 60. Every time one side gets two levels taller than the other, rotate.
  4. Count the height of your tree and compare with a plain stick of 6.

Think about it. Why is a stick of 1000 numbers so much slower to search than a balanced tree of 1000?
