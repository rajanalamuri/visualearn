Sieve of Eratosthenes

Starter · ages 8+

A prime is a number whose only factors are 1 and itself. More than 2,000 years ago Eratosthenes found them by crossing out multiples. The numbers left uncrossed are prime.

Play this lesson

How it works

  1. write the numbers 2 … N
  2. for p = 2, 3, … while p×p ≤ N
  3. if p not crossed out: prime
  4. cross out p×p, p×p+p, … ≤ N
  5. every number left is prime
  • InventedAbout 240 BC
  • Stops at√N
  • WorkO(N log log N)

Sieve of Eratosthenes Starter

The hundred square

You need Paper, a pencil and three or four colours · Time 20 minutes

  1. Draw a 10 by 10 grid and write the numbers 1 to 100, ten to a row.
  2. Shade 1. It is not prime.
  3. Circle 2. With one colour, shade every second number after it: 4, 6, 8 …
  4. Circle 3, the next number that is not shaded. With a new colour, shade every third number after it.
  5. Keep going with 5 and 7. Every number still not shaded is prime. Circle them all.

Think about it. You could stop after 7. Why do you never need to cross out with 11 when you go up to 100?
