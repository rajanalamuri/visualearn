Euclid's GCD

Starter · ages 8+

The greatest common divisor is the biggest number that divides both numbers. Euclid found it by cutting the biggest squares he could off a rectangle, again and again, until nothing was left.

Play this lesson

How it works

  1. gcd(a, b) // a × b rectangle
  2. while b > 0
  3. k ← how many b×b fit in a
  4. cut off k squares
  5. a, b ← b, a − k×b
  6. return a
  • InventedAbout 300 BC
  • UsesFractions, gears
  • WorkO(log n)

Try it on paper

Euclid's GCD Starter

Squares from a sheet

You need Squared paper, scissors and a pencil · Time 15 minutes

  1. Cut a rectangle 21 squares long and 13 squares wide out of squared paper.
  2. Fold the short side down to make the biggest square you can. Cut it off.
  3. Do the same with the strip that is left, and keep going.
  4. Write down the size of every square you cut. The last, smallest square is the GCD.
  5. Try again with 12 by 8. Can tiles of the smallest size fill the whole first rectangle?

Think about it. With 21 by 13 your squares were 13, 8, 5, 3, 2, 1, 1. Each number is the sum of the next two. Can you find a rectangle where every cut makes exactly one square?
