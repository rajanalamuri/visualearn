Approximation

Builder · ages 10+

Some problems are too hard to solve perfectly in time. A good guess that is fast and never too far off is useful. Guard roads by putting guards at both ends of an unguarded road. You never use more than twice the best.

Play this lesson

How it works

  1. guards ← none
  2. pick any road with no guard at either end
  3. put guards at both ends
  4. now every road touching those towns is guarded
  5. repeat until every road is guarded
  • GivesNearly the best
  • PromiseAt most twice as many
  • SpeedFast

Guard the roads

You need Paper and a pencil, and a small map of 6 towns and 7 roads · Time 15 minutes

  1. Draw 6 towns and 7 roads. A guard in a town protects every road that touches it.
  2. Pick any unguarded road. Put guards at both its ends. Cross out every road they now protect.
  3. Repeat until all roads are crossed out. Count the guards.
  4. Try to find a smaller set by thinking. Is yours less than half of what the quick method used?

Think about it. Why can the best possible answer never use fewer than half as many guards as this quick method?
