Bellman-Ford

Advanced · ages 13+

Dijkstra needs every road to cost something. Bellman-Ford can handle a road that gives you a refund. It tries every road again and again, and keeps any shorter way it finds, until nothing changes.

Play this lesson

How it works

  1. start: 0 for Home, ∞ for every other town
  2. repeat up to (towns − 1) times:
  3. for every road u → v of length w:
  4. if dist[u] + w < dist[v]: update dist[v]
  5. if a whole round changes nothing: stop
  • HandlesNegative roads
  • TimeO(towns × roads)
  • RoundsUp to towns − 1

Try it on paper

Bellman-Ford Advanced

Try every road

You need Paper, a pencil and a small map with one negative road · Time 15 minutes

  1. Draw 5 towns and some one-way roads with lengths. Make one road a refund, like −2.
  2. Write 0 by Home and ∞ by the others.
  3. Go through every road in any order. If dist[start] + length beats dist[end], cross out and write the better number.
  4. Go through all roads again. Stop when a whole pass changes nothing.

Think about it. Why can the answer never need more than (towns − 1) rounds?
